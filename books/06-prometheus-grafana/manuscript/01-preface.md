# About This Book

This book is the sixth volume of the DevOps Handbook Series. *Docker for DevOps Engineers* answered how to package an application, *Kubernetes for DevOps Engineers* answered how to run it, *GitHub Actions for DevOps Engineers* answered how to build, test, and ship it, *Ansible for DevOps Engineers* answered how to configure it, and *Terraform for DevOps Engineers* answered how to provision the infrastructure underneath it. This volume answers the question that arrives the first morning after your first production deploy: how do you *know* the platform is healthy - and how do you find out before your users do? Prometheus and Grafana are the most widely deployed answer to that question, and the pair whose metrics-first, pull-based model fits most naturally alongside the cloud native platforms covered in the earlier books.

The book is organized in two parts. Part I, Prometheus Essentials, builds the vocabulary and the muscle memory: the monitoring landscape and where the three signals fit, Prometheus architecture and the dimensional data model, installation and your first scrape, the exporter ecosystem and instrumenting your own code, PromQL from first selectors through recording rules and subqueries, service discovery and the relabeling pipeline, and alerting with Alertmanager - routing trees, silences, inhibition, and the design of alerts that are worth waking someone up. Part II, Grafana and Production Practice, is the working platform team's part: Grafana fundamentals and first dashboards, dashboard design that survives contact with an incident, unified alerting in Grafana, running Prometheus at scale - high availability, remote write, Thanos and Mimir, federation, and sizing - and monitoring Kubernetes itself with the Prometheus Operator and kube-prometheus-stack. The parts are sequential; Part II assumes only Part I and the earlier chapters of Part II itself.

The content reflects the platform as of October 2026. The current stable lines are Prometheus 3.15 (the 3.x series began in November 2024), Alertmanager 0.34, and Grafana 13.3, with node_exporter at 1.12 and Grafana Mimir at 3.2. Three generational changes shape the examples in this book. First, Prometheus 3 replaced the old web UI, added Remote Write 2.0, made UTF-8 metric and label names the default, and shipped a native OTLP receiver, so OpenTelemetry SDKs can write metrics to Prometheus directly; the book treats these as facts of life rather than experiments. Second, Grafana 13 made dynamic dashboards and Git Sync generally available, which moves dashboards from hand-built artifacts to code-reviewed, Git-managed resources - Chapter 10 is built around that workflow. Third, the long-term-storage ecosystem settled into a small number of durable choices, so the scale chapter compares Thanos and Mimir as architectures rather than as news. Where a capability is experimental, enterprise-only, or plan-dependent, the book says so explicitly instead of presenting the happy path as universal.

Three conventions run through the book. Commands, configuration, and PromQL appear in monospaced blocks, and every example is shown complete enough to paste into a project and run - the Docker Compose stack in Chapter 3 grows with you through the book. Technical terms such as scraper, exporter, series, panel, and contact point are kept in English and capitalized when they name a platform concept, matching how professionals speak. Notes marked **Best practice** condense operational wisdom, while **Watch out** boxes flag the mistakes that cause the most incidents, from `rate()` windows that are too short through unbounded label cardinality to alert rules without a `for` clause. Because the book targets practitioners, full labs appear in the five chapters where hands-on practice changes the most: a working Prometheus plus node_exporter plus Grafana stack in Docker Compose, instrumenting an application and probing with blackbox_exporter, a PromQL drill with recording rules, the complete alert pipeline from rule to delivered notification, and kube-prometheus-stack on a kind cluster. The appendices compress the rest into a desk reference: a PromQL and CLI cheat sheet, a catalog of the exporters worth knowing, and a troubleshooting guide organized by symptom.

Every chapter closes with a *See also* list, every internal reference is a live link, and the Part dividers, table of contents, and headings are navigable in all three editions - in the PDF the contents carry computed page numbers and the heading tree appears in the outline panel.
