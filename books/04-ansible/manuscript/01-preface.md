# About This Book

This book is the fourth volume of the DevOps Handbook Series. *Docker for DevOps Engineers* answered how to package an application, *Kubernetes for DevOps Engineers* answered how to run it, and *GitHub Actions for DevOps Engineers* answered how to build, test, and ship it automatically. This volume answers the next question in the platform engineering stack: how do you configure, change, and operate the software and the infrastructure underneath it, repeatably, on hundreds or thousands of machines, without a runbook and a shell alias doing the work by hand? Ansible is the most widely deployed answer to that question, and the one whose agentless, YAML-first model fits most naturally alongside the container platforms covered in the earlier books.

The book is organized in two parts. Part I, Ansible Essentials, builds the vocabulary and the muscle memory: the execution model and why idempotency is the load-bearing idea, installation and project layout, inventories and host patterns, the module universe and ad-hoc commands, playbook anatomy with handlers and tags, variables and facts and Jinja2 templating, conditionals and failure control, and roles and collections as the unit of reuse. Part II, Advanced Operations, is the working platform team's part: secrets management with Vault, execution environments and the modern toolchain, performance at scale, testing with ansible-lint and Molecule, automation platforms from AWX to Ansible Automation Platform and Event-Driven Ansible, and the fleet-scale patterns - rolling updates, delegation, hybrid estates - that hold production together. The parts are sequential; Part II assumes only Part I and the earlier chapters of Part II itself.

The content reflects the platform as of October 2026. The current stable line is ansible-core 2.21 (general availability in May 2026, currently at 2.21.5), shipped by the Ansible community package 14, whose minor releases land roughly every four weeks. Two generational changes shape the examples in this book. First, the templating overhaul and Data Tagging introduced in ansible-core 2.19 changed how values flow through playbooks and how mistakes are reported, so the variable chapters treat lazy evaluation and type tags as facts of life rather than surprises. Second, the platform around the tool consolidated: execution environments became the default way to run Ansible, the `awx.awx` collection left the community package while AWX itself modernized upstream, and Red Hat shipped Ansible Automation Platform 2.6 and 2.7 with the automation orchestrator and a self-service portal. Where a capability is Red Hat-specific, a preview, or plan-dependent, the book says so explicitly instead of presenting the happy path as universal.

Three conventions run through the book. Commands and YAML appear in monospaced blocks, and every playbook is shown complete enough to paste into a project and run. Technical terms such as playbook, task, handler, role, and execution environment are kept in English and capitalized when they name a platform concept, matching how professionals speak. Notes marked **Best practice** condense operational wisdom, while **Watch out** boxes flag the mistakes that cause the most incidents, from `command: sudo` through untemplated passwords to the mutation of a variable you are still looping over. Because the book targets practitioners, full labs appear in the six chapters where hands-on practice changes the most: your first playbook against container targets, dynamic inventory, a templated service with handlers, conditionals and failure control, a role and collection refactor, and vault-protected automation wired into CI. The appendices compress the rest into a desk reference: a configuration and CLI cheat sheet, four starter automation artifacts you can adopt today, and a troubleshooting guide organized by symptom.

Every chapter closes with a *See also* list, every internal reference is a live link, and the Part dividers, table of contents, and headings are navigable in all three editions - in the PDF the contents carry computed page numbers and the heading tree appears in the outline panel.
